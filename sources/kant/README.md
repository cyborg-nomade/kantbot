# Kant source texts

This directory contains the public-domain text references used by the project:

- [First-edition text](critique-a.md), the body of the *Critique* with A-page
  anchors from A1 through A856;
- [Second-edition excerpts](second-edition-excerpts.md), B1-5 and B294-315;
- [A Deduction](deduction-a.md), A95-A130, from the 1781 first edition;
- [B Deduction](deduction-b.md), B129-B169, from the 1787 second edition.

The focused deduction files remain convenient reading copies even though the A
Deduction also occurs within the complete first-edition text.

## Edition and provenance

The English text is Immanuel Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, translated by F.
Max Müller. Müller's translation was first published in 1881 and revised in
1896; the digital source reproduces the 1922 second revised edition published by
Macmillan. Its main text follows the first edition of Kant's *Critique*, while
material introduced or rewritten for the second edition appears in numbered
supplements. The B Deduction is Supplement XIV.

The transcription was obtained from the [Online Library of Liberty
edition](https://oll.libertyfund.org/titles/ller-critique-of-pure-reason?html=true).
The [A text begins in Section
II](https://oll.libertyfund.org/titles/ller-critique-of-pure-reason?html=true#lf0330_label_181),
and the [B text begins in Supplement
XIV](https://oll.libertyfund.org/titles/ller-critique-of-pure-reason?html=true#lf0330_label_465).

## Rights and local transformations

Kant's text and Müller's translation are in the public domain. The source
edition predates 1923, and Müller died in 1900. No copyright is claimed over the
reproduced text in this directory.

The local Markdown copies were mechanically extracted from the source EPUB.
They preserve the wording, paragraph order, section headings, italics, and the
square brackets by which Müller marks first-edition material omitted from the
second edition. Named HTML anchors (for example, `a103`, `b151`, and `b306`)
make standard A/B citations linkable from other repository documents. The full
A text retains every page boundary exposed by the digital source; seven uncited
page boundaries are absent there. Because the digital B-edition supplements do
not expose each B page break, their anchors mark standard section starts or
identifiable paragraph starts; a few links therefore open the section
containing the cited page. The extraction removed:

- web and ebook markup;
- visible source-edition page furniture (A-page positions survive as named anchors);
- the source edition's translator/editor footnote markers; and
- an ornamental divider at the end of Supplement XIV.

Consult the linked source edition for its footnotes, textual apparatus, and
facsimile. These nineteenth-century translations are useful as openly
reproducible primary texts, but interpretive work should compare their wording
with the German and, where available, a current scholarly translation.
